Node 20 is past end of support — find every pin in the repo

Official Node.js 20 support ended 30 April 2026. Updating .nvmrc is not enough if a Dockerfile or workflow still pins 20.

Node.js 20 (codename Iron) reached official end of support on 30 April 2026. That date is the end field in the nodejs/Release schedule.json, fetched for this guide on 10 November 2026. On that date, the even-numbered lines still in the schedule as supported LTS releases were 22, 24, and 26.

Updating .nvmrc is usually the first step people remember. It is rarely the only place the old major still appears.

Places to look

  • Version files: .nvmrc, .node-version, the nodejs line in .tool-versions
  • Manifests: engines in package.json, Volta’s Node field, devEngines.runtime
  • Docker: every FROM node:… line, including builder stages, and Compose image: node:…
  • CI: node-version on actions/setup-node, matrix lists, and any hardcoded version in other CI config (Cloud Build, Circle, and so on)
  • Hosted runtimes: serverless settings, PaaS config, and container orchestration that install Node independently of the Dockerfile

A floating tag such as node:20 or node:lts is not the same as a supported runtime. 20 will stay 20 after the line is end-of-life. lts can move without anyone changing a file, which is a different kind of surprise.

Check, then bump

Search alone is easy to under-count. A small CI gate that reads the same files and fails when they disagree — or when a pin is past official end of support — catches the one you missed. RuntimeGate’s --eol flag does that with a vendored copy of the official dates (snapshot 10 November 2026). It does not replace reading the schedule when you cut a release; the table can go stale.

This guide is not telling you which supported LTS to move to. It is telling you to find every declaration, keep them in agreement, and stop recommending Node 20 in examples now that official support has ended.
